Gameplay Mechanics

Kindergarten is a puzzle and adventure game centered on a student's daily attendance at school, where the environment is subtly but consistently unsettling. The faculty exhibit peculiar behaviors, the students are unpredictable, and the sequence of events rarely remains consistent. Beneath its vibrant, child-like aesthetic, the game conceals a series of unusual mysteries, high-risk scenarios, and events presented with darkly comedic undertones that unravel through player choices and exploration. Each interaction holds significance, and determining the optimal sequence of actions is essential for discovering the truth behind the school's extraordinary nature.

Controls and Interface

The control scheme is straight forward:
Movement: W (up), A (left), S (down), D (right)
Interaction: Mouse Click
Wait: Pressing Enter allows the player to advance time without performing an action
Esc: open the menu

The Development Team

The game Kindergarten was created by Connor Boyle and Sean Young, two independent developers operating under the name SmashGames. Their development objective was to deliver a unique interactive experience, combining elements of dark humor, adventure gaming, and puzzle-solving, all situated within a peculiar kindergarten setting characterized by its mysteries and absurd scenarios.

Kindergarten gameplay

The Kindergarten narrative is presented across three separate games, each corresponding to a different day of the school day. The first installment takes place on Monday, which appears to be a day of extensive challenges and repetition.

This day is structured into 6 distinct segments:

  • The Room
  • School Yard
  • Morning Time
  • Lunch
  • Recess
  • Show and Tell

The Player's Room

The Player's Room is the preparatory stage where the player determines which key item to bring to school for the day's events.

Selectable Items from the Room

  • Cindy's Flower
  • Key Mold
  • Lunch Pass
  • Teacher's Phone
  • Billy's Note
  • Principal's Room Key

These six are the primary items, generally acquired as rewards for completing mission objectives for other characters.

In addition to these, two other categories of items are available.

The Piggy Bank

This is a unique interactive object that cannot be physically transported to school. The player engages with it to monitor current funds and allocate the amount of money to carry for the school day.

The Piggy Bank is a storage mechanism. It has a monetary capacity limit of $10.00.

Financial System

The player can acquire currency daily through various means: theft, earned rewards, or by selling goods to the character known as Monty (discussed further below).

Procedural Constraints

Following the item selection in the Player's Room, the player proceeds to the kindergarten setting. This transition marks the beginning of the strategic decision-making required for the day's progress.

Unrestricted activity is not permitted. The time available for action is finite. Progression is regulated by "action points," which are visually represented by apples.

A majority of in-game actions consume these action points, including specific character conversations, commerce (buying and selling), and aggressive actions toward the Janitor.

With these prerequisites established, the experience of Kindergarten commences!

The School Yard

This area serves as the initial primary location for engaging with most of the game's non-player characters.

Characters

One of the first individuals encountered is:

Buggs

Buggs functions as the class's aggressive figure. His demeanor is influenced by factors such as his physical appearance, his social isolation, and the absence of his father.

Standard Encounter Protocol

Upon arriving at kindergarten, he assesses the player's capital. If the player possesses more than $3.00, he will confiscate fifty percent of the funds; otherwise, the interaction results in physical confrontation.

Miss Applegate

She is the classroom instructor. Her professional conduct is highly questionable. She has demonstrated the capacity to lethally respond to stress, may solicit the player's aid in removing other students (an element discussed later), and exhibits signs of substance dependence.

Mr. Janitor

He is the designated maintenance worker for the facility. This role masks a more sinister identity: he is a known killer of minors, a veteran of military service, and is subject to a legal restraining order.

Interaction with him in the bathroom may result in one of two outcomes: a lethal attack on the player, or a favorable negotiation with the Lunch Lady to reduce the price of a chocolate bar at lunchtime from $10 to under $1.

Nugget

He is a conspicuously unusual student who refers to himself in the third person. He is emotionally distressed due to the disappearance of his single close friend. If the player initiates conversation regarding his missing friend, the interaction will lead to mandatory reporting to the Principal's office.

Cindy

A student characterized by manipulative and antagonistic behavior, typically identified by her pink attire. She is the class's dominant female figure, exhibiting self-centered and spiteful traits. If the player engages with her, she will issue a request to sabotage Lily's hair with chewing gum during class. Successful completion of this task results in the player being designated as her romantic partner.

Lily

She is an introverted student who avoids contact due to distress over her brother's disappearance. She strongly suspects the Principal's involvement in the incident but lacks concrete evidence to support her claim.

Jerome

He is the influential student who dictates social standing based on superficial assessments. Initial interaction is characterized by rejection, citing the player is not cool. His elevated status is attributed to being the Principal's son and having stolen a significant item from the Principal's private office.

Monty

He operates as a small-scale vendor, offering a diverse array of merchandise ranging from simple toys to illicit substances. His inventory during the School Yard segment includes:

  • A yo-yo: $1.00
  • A package of cigarettes: $1.00
  • A voice recording device: $2.50
  • A screwdriver: $1.00

His available goods change during the Lunch period:

  • A salad: $0.75
  • A McGloves Silly Meal: $2.50

During Recess, his inventory is limited to a single item: a Monstermon trading card for $12.50.

The Hall Monitor

This character is primarily a mechanism for enforcing school rules, which frequently results in the player being directed to the Principal's office. He is a young adult with undisclosed activities outside of school hours. Offering him cigarettes can result in a momentary lapse of duty, preventing him from reporting the player's unauthorized movement.

The Principal

The administrator of the kindergarten, who is a primary subject of suspicion. While outwardly conventional, there are indications of underlying irregularities. Lily's accusations may hold merit or they may be a distraction. Regardless, being sent to his office is consistently an undesirable consequence.


The Morning Time

The Morning Time segment begins once all action points have been expended.The player can then explore the classroom environment and interact with the assembled students. This period offers opportunities for further character engagement and the execution of specific tasks.
